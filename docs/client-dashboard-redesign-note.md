# Client Dashboard Redesign Note

## Why the new version is better

The previous dashboard exposed useful information, but it felt more like a collection of cards than a clear client command center. Important actions such as check-ins, plan access, and navigation between major coaching areas competed visually instead of being prioritized by urgency.

The redesigned version is better because it gives the client a clearer order of attention:

1. The top of the screen now acts like a real home base, with a stronger hero section and immediate context for the day.
2. The most important action, especially a due check-in, is surfaced higher and more explicitly instead of being buried lower in the page.
3. Core destinations such as Tracker, Plan, Check-in, Journey, League, and Coach Chat are grouped into a dedicated quick-access area rather than being scattered across unrelated cards.
4. Status information such as subscription timing, coach assignment, and plan readiness is separated from action areas, which reduces clutter and makes scanning easier.
5. The redesign works better on mobile because it favors short decision paths, larger tap targets, and clearer sectioning above the fold.

## Specific improvements over the previous layout

- Better hierarchy: urgent actions are more visible, and supporting details are moved lower.
- Better scanability: the page is divided into clear sections with stronger headings and fewer mixed-purpose cards.
- Better action flow: clients can move from overview to plan, tracker, check-in, journey, or coach chat with fewer decisions.
- Better mobile behavior: the most relevant actions are easier to discover without hunting through the page.

## Local polish added during review

During local review, the due check-in experience was improved further:

- The dedicated `Check-in due` card now appears before the quick-access grid.
- The quick-access grid also promotes `Check-in` to the first slot whenever a check-in is due.

This makes the dashboard more responsive to what the client actually needs to do next, instead of showing every destination with equal priority.
